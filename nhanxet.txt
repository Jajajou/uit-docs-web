1. Overall Impression (2–3 sentences)
When a normal student lands on the UIT Portal, the first impression is that they have entered an internal administrative dashboard, not a friendly AI assistant. The interface feels dense, technical, and slightly intimidating — filled with status badges, pending-review warnings, extraction confidence metrics, and approval timelines that have nothing to do with a student simply asking “Học phí kỳ 2 năm nay bao nhiêu?”. Compared to Grok.com or ChatGPT in 2026, this feels outdated and enterprise-heavy — more like a 2022 internal tool than a premium AI product students would actually enjoy using.
2. Strengths (list 6–8 bullet points)

Clear separation of the three main workflows (Chat, Upload, Manager) in the top navigation.
The Conversation Index on the left provides a useful history of past student questions with quick visual indicators (alerts, references, warnings).
The central chat area correctly displays user/assistant messages with timestamps and inline “Confidence 72%” badge.
References and Warnings are explicitly surfaced (good for transparency in a university RAG system).
Upload workspace has a clean drag-and-drop zone and helpful guidelines for lecturers.
Workspace Manager cards are well-organized with clear status icons (green check, blue “C”, red “X”) and timestamps.
The system already attempts to show citations and source metadata, which is essential for a university AI.
Role-based top-right “Admin” dropdown indicates proper permission awareness.

3. Major Weaknesses & Pain Points
Layout & Visual Hierarchy

The chat page is heavily fragmented: left sidebar + massive central transcript + dense right sidebar creates three competing columns with no clear focal point.
Chat area feels squeezed and secondary instead of being the hero of the page.

Information Overload & Redundancy

Too many boxes, labels, counters (“MESSAGES 2”, “REFERENCED SOURCES 1”, “WARNINGS 1”), and metadata panels compete for attention.
Repetitive information (e.g., the same reference appears in “Reference stack”, “Conversation transcript”, and warning banner).

Public vs Internal Information Exposure

Critical flaw: internal admin details are leaking into the public-facing chat (Latest answer health, Warning contract, low_confidence banner, “Public chat should always surface uncertainty…”).
Students see “Pending review”, “Tài liệu nguồn đang ở trạng thái pending review”, and “Approval timeline” — information that should never reach end users.

Chat Experience

The “Conversation transcript” section looks like a debug log rather than a natural conversation.
“Mock message” box at the bottom is clearly for developers/testers and should never be visible to students.
No modern chat input feel — the input area is small and buried.

Typography, Spacing & Modern Aesthetics

Heavy use of small, tight text and multiple gray/blue/yellow alert boxes creates visual fatigue.
Lack of generous whitespace and breathing room typical of 2026 AI interfaces.

Admin/Workflow Elements that leak into public view

Entire right sidebar (Latest answer health, Reference stack, Warning contract) and Upload/Manager tabs are visible and feel completely out of place for students.

Mobile responsiveness (if inferable)

Three-column layout will almost certainly collapse badly on mobile; no indication of responsive design.

4. Information That Is Redundant for a Normal Student User
The following elements should be completely hidden from regular students:

“Public answer layer”, “Avg confidence 72%”
Right sidebar: “Latest answer health”, “Confidence 72%”, “1 warnings”, “1 references”
“Reference stack” + full document cards (“Thong bao hoc phi hoc ky 2”, “Pending review”)
“Warning contract” box + “low_confidence” banner + explanatory text about “Public chat should always surface uncertainty…”
“Conversation transcript” timestamps and full internal metadata
“Tài liệu nguồn đang ở trạng thái pending review” yellow banner
All “Draft readiness 0/5”, “Approval timeline”, “Temporal extraction”, “Extraction confidence”, “Human approval”, “Visibility release”, “Latest extraction result”, “DOCUMENT TYPE: other”, “INDEXED AT: Pending”, etc.
“Mock message” section and “Send mock message” button
Any mention of “Lecturer intake”, “Internal account rule”, “Editorial metadata”

5. Comparison with Grok.com Style (2026 standard)
The current UIT Portal is very far from Grok.com’s clean 2026 aesthetic. Grok.com uses:

Single focused chat area that occupies ~80% of the screen
Minimal left sidebar (history only)
Almost zero persistent right sidebar
Generous whitespace, large typography, soft shadows, and modern rounded input bar
Citations appear as clean superscript numbers or small inline pills that expand on click
No visible internal confidence metrics or “pending review” warnings unless explicitly requested

UIT Portal currently feels like a backend admin panel that accidentally has a chat box in the middle, while Grok.com feels like a premium AI companion.
6. Quick-Win Improvements (Top 8 recommendations)

Hide the entire right sidebar on the public Chat page (move to admin-only toggle).
Remove “Public answer layer”, “Avg confidence”, and all warning banners from student view.
Replace “Conversation transcript” with a clean, modern chat thread (remove timestamps unless requested).
Make the chat input box much larger, centered, and Grok-style (with suggestion chips above it).
Move Upload and Manager tabs into a separate “Staff Portal” section accessible only after role-based login.
Replace yellow/red alert boxes with subtle, non-intrusive indicators only when truly necessary.
Increase whitespace dramatically around the chat area.
Add a prominent “New chat” button at the top of the left sidebar and make conversation cards cleaner.

7. Recommended New Layout Structure
Ideal Chat page (main public-facing page):

Top navigation: UIT Portal logo | Chat (active) | Knowledge Base | My Documents (optional) | [Staff Portal – visible only to lecturers/admins]
Left sidebar (collapsible):
New chat button
Search conversations
Clean list of past threads with title + date only

Main area (70–75% of screen):
Clean chat thread (user on right, assistant on left, Grok-style bubbles)
Assistant messages include small superscript citation numbers
At the bottom of each assistant reply: subtle “View sources” button (opens a clean drawer)

Bottom input bar: Large, modern, full-width with attachment icon and voice input (optional)
Right side: Nothing by default. Only a temporary drawer that slides in when user clicks “View sources” or “Details” (showing references + confidence only if student wants to see it).

This layout keeps the university’s need for citations and source transparency while making the experience feel like a premium 2026 AI product that students will actually love using.
